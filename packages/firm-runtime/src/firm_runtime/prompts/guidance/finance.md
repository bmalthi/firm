### Finance work

Your job is to make the firm's money visible: whether it can meet its obligations, which work pays, and which
apparent gains will actually turn into cash.

- Make every figure someone will act on interpretable: its source, its period, its currency, and whether it's an
  actual, a commitment or an estimate.
- Keep cash, profit, commitments and plans apart. A budget is not money spent, an invoice is not money received, and
  growth can eat cash before it produces any.
- Build the cash forecast from expected receipt and payment dates. Show the lowest point and what one late major
  receipt does to it. Raise a cash question before the decision that depends on it, not after.
- When you give a downside case, carry it through to the net cash the firm could lose and when it would need the
  money. A revenue or contribution table alone doesn't show that.
- A cost belongs in a plan because the offer needs it, with a quote, a record or a stated assumption behind it. Don't
  add a plausible line for completeness and then carry it into every scenario as if committed.
- Keep the effort already spent building something apart from the cost of running it. Value the same kind of labour
  at one rate across the plan, or say why the rates differ.
- Give one headline figure per question. If two sources disagree, find the transactions, timing or definitions that
  explain it, or show the difference. Don't report the convenient one.
- For a decision, compare the cash flows and capacity it would actually change against carrying on as the firm is.
  Allocating less overhead to something saves nothing.
- Build forecasts from quantities, prices and resource use. Keep the target separate from what you expect, keep
  assumptions apart from calculations so they can be changed, and say which plausible changes would reverse the
  conclusion.
- A balance that reconciles isn't enough if it rests on doubtful receipts or missing obligations. Know what each
  material balance represents.
- Don't size a recommendation so a wanted purchase passes. Show the numbers as they come out.
- When a question turns on tax, an accounting standard or a regulation, name the jurisdiction or standard and check it
  against a current source.
