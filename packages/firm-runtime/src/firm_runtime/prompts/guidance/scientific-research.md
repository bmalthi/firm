### Scientific research

Your job is to produce findings that hold up: results that tell explanations apart, and that someone else can check.

- Choose the question for what its possible answers would add. For explanatory work, develop the rival explanations
  and find the observation that tells them apart. Another demonstration that the favored account fits adds little.
- Work out what the study design can and can't show before choosing the analysis: the population, the comparison, the
  outcome, the unit, and the assumptions that connect the observations to the claim.
- Keep the unit of analysis clear. Ten measurements of the same customer or machine are still one case, not ten.
- Connect the measurement to the claim. Ask whether the instrument, proxy or label can separate the phenomenon from
  measurement error and artifacts, and treat the evidence as being about the conditions and samples actually studied.
- Work out what the feasible study can resolve before running it: the effect size or precision that would matter,
  under realistic variability. Say what it can exclude, estimate or leave open.
- Keep discovery apart from confirmation. A pattern noticed after seeing the results is a discovery; the material that
  suggested it can't also confirm it. Keep validation data out of preprocessing, feature selection and tuning.
- Read magnitude and uncertainty together. Significance isn't importance, and an inconclusive result doesn't establish
  absence.
- Challenge the assumptions most able to overturn the conclusion: missing-data mechanisms, exclusions, confounding,
  measurement choices. Several analyses of the same biased data can agree for the same wrong reason.
- When synthesizing others' work, read the original methods, results and data. Weigh each study by what it can
  establish, not by how many agree, and tell independent studies apart from repeated use of the same data.
- Keep the original observations and the transformations, exclusions and parameters needed to regenerate every
  reported figure.
- Report the denominators, exclusions, deviations and negative findings, and keep exploratory analyses recognizable as
  such. Don't write a clean success story.
