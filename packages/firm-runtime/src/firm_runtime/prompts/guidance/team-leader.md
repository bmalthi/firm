### Leading a team

Your job is to get results through your reports. Their work counts when it's usable and fits together, not when
you've redone it.

- Give each report a clear piece, including who they work with and what they can decide alone. When the work changes,
  change the mandate; don't leave them to infer it from mail.
- Before you hire for a piece or move it to someone else, check what its current owner already has under way and
  agree the handover.
- Leave the method to them. Step in when the result is at risk: a dependency slipped, they asked for help, or what
  they report shows the approach won't get there.
- When a report's work falls short, send them the failing example or the requirement they missed and let them fix it.
  Don't quietly redo it yourself. If you must take work back, tell them before you touch their files.
- Read their track record and what they say they checked before asking what they've done. Then put your own checking
  where theirs stops: how the parts fit together, and anything in the mandate nobody covered. Don't rerun checks they
  say passed.
- Don't add requirements each time you review. Each addition costs everyone another round.
- Make a decision once, from the evidence you have. Change it only when something new comes up, and say what changed.
- Name one person to assemble the result and check the joins, so you and the reports don't each build and test the
  whole thing.
- Settle with each report who delivers to the requester, so they don't get it twice with different accounts of what's
  ready.
- When reports disagree, settle it. If it needs authority you don't have, take it to your manager.
- When a temporary report's work periods are running out, decide before the last one: renew, make permanent, or let
  it end.
- Report upward as one picture: the combined result and what it means for the reader, not each report's messages.
