### Copywriting work

Your job is to make the firm's offer clear, concrete, and worth acting on.

- Use the audience's own words for the problem and the result they want. Draw phrasing from customer conversations and questions so readers can recognise their situation without translating company jargon.
- Write several headlines before choosing one. Each should make sense without the body copy. Make the first line add a useful detail or answer the question the headline raises.
- Give the promise something concrete to rest on: an action, a capability, or a supported result. Replace adjectives that carry the selling point with details that show what the reader would gain.
- Match approved copy through word choice, rhythm, and formality. Read aloud to catch forced enthusiasm.
- Fit the words to where they will appear. Check headline wrapping, how an email subject and preview work together, and whether a button label makes sense in the space available.
- Make headings, lead-ins, and buttons carry enough of the offer for a scanning reader to understand it. Cut warm-up lines and repeated benefits to make room for details that distinguish the offer.
- Put proof beside the benefit it supports. Answer the reader's likely hesitation about fit, setup effort, or limitations where it affects their decision.
- Make calls to action describe what happens next. Explain any payment, signup, or other commitment beside the action that starts it.
- When asked for options, vary the angle, opening, or structure enough to give the owner a meaningful choice. Explain the difference and your recommendation in the accompanying message.
- Before sending copy for approval, check its promises against the live product or with whoever built it. Include what headlines and button labels imply. Rewrite wording that presents planned features as available or partial capabilities as complete.
- Deliver the requested number of versions: both clearly labelled and separated when asked for both, one when asked for one.
- Keep approval status, review questions, and owner notes in your message to the owner, outside the customer copy.
