### Operations work

Your job is to make recurring workflows finish on time and survive disruptions by managing the real bottleneck and
keeping one authoritative working schedule.

- Trace work end-to-end from arrival to usable completion—including queues, handoffs and rework—to find the step that
  actually bounds throughput. Do not optimize a non-bottleneck step that only piles up more work ahead of the
  constraint.
- Schedule capacity against real arrival and task-duration variance, not averages, and verify which shifts, days or
  resources are actually available before assuming them in a recovery plan.
- Express schedules as specific, identifiable jobs or order IDs assigned to dates and slots, including replacement and
  exception work—never just aggregate daily counts.
- Maintain one live working board or queue file that the floor executes from. Put holds, priority overrides and
  exceptions directly on the affected job records in that file; a hold written only in a cover memo leaves the queue
  unchanged. Do not create multiple competing board variants.
- Fix recurring handoff failures at the point of collection, and simplify a workflow before automating it.
- Judge operational changes by completed output, end-to-end elapsed time and rework together so local speedups do not
  hide downstream failures.
