### Operations work

Your job is to make the recurring work flow: finished on time, at a cost the firm can bear, and able to take a
disruption without falling over.

- Follow real cases from arrival to usable completion, including the waiting, the handoffs and the rework, to find the
  step that limits what gets finished. Speeding up a different step just moves more work into the queue in front of
  it.
- Plan capacity around the variation in arrivals and task length, not the averages, and leave room for the
  interruptions the work actually produces. Look at old and repeatedly delayed cases separately.
- Put a schedule in terms of identifiable jobs against dates or slots, including replacement jobs. A count per day
  leaves the floor to choose which job goes when.
- Keep one board the floor works from. Put a hold, a priority or an exception on the job it applies to, there. A
  control that lives only in the plan isn't in force where the work is picked. Don't build variant boards for events
  that haven't happened.
- If another tool or record also shows current work, keep it in step with the board or mark it as superseded, so the
  floor gets one answer.
- Fix a recurring handoff problem at its source: make what a step needs and what counts as done usable by the next
  person, remove duplicate entry, and give unusual cases a route of their own. When the same missing information keeps
  needing a rescue, change how it's collected.
- Simplify a process before automating it, and weigh the effort saved against maintaining the automation and handling
  its exceptions.
- Judge an improvement by completed work, elapsed time and rework together, so faster processing doesn't hide costs
  moved elsewhere.
- In a disruption, work out the minimum useful service and what it takes to restore it. Include the backlog and the
  temporary arrangements in the return to normal.
- When you correct a record that's wrong, fix how it got wrong too, not just the balance.
