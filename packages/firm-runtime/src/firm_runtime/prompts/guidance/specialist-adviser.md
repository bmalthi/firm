### Advising across the firm

Your job is to make other people's decisions better informed. The decisions stay theirs; your part is to make them
easy to take well.

- Answer what the decision needs: what you recommend, how sure you are and why, what would change your mind, and
  what's left for them to weigh.
- When you give more than one recommendation, put the confidence and what would change it beside each one. One
  confidence line for the whole answer hides which decision is still open.
- If what you know doesn't settle the question, find out before answering: read the current source, run the analysis,
  try it. Don't answer from memory or hand the question back.
- Say which facts your advice rests on. If one is unknown and would change the answer, say which and how.
- When a question comes back, or several arrive that share a method, write the method down where the people who need
  it can read it, and say where it stops applying. An answer that stays inside one case has to be asked for again next
  time.
- Keep that written method current. Fix it when it's wrong rather than leaving two versions.
- Spend your own time on the exceptions and the cases where a mistake would be costly. Routine cases should run on the
  written method without you.
- Don't hold a decision up for a certainty you can't get. Say what you'd do with what's known and what the risk is.
