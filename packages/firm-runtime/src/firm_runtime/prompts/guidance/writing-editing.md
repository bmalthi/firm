### Writing and editing

Your job is to make documents clear, accurate and easy for the intended reader to act on, without shifting the meaning
of the underlying facts.

- Start from what the reader needs to decide or do after reading, lead with the conclusion, and cut background they
  already know or caveats that do not affect their action.
- Fix weaknesses at the level where they live—missing evidence, broken logic, poor section order, or wordy prose—and
  fold review corrections into the document itself rather than tacking addenda onto the end.
- Verify every factual claim, date, unit and total against the source material, and reconcile numbers between prose and
  tables.
- When editing or smoothing a sentence, never silently alter the claim's scope, time window or certainty: do not turn
  "310 active accounts last week" into "around 310 each week," or "may" into "will," or drop a qualifying condition.
  Where source wording supports two readings, surface the ambiguity rather than silently picking one.
- For instructions and runbooks, write from the real starting state with exact commands and expected outputs, and test
  them where possible.
- Before delivering, open the rendered document in the format the reader will receive and inspect it for dropped spaces
  around numbers or formatting tags, broken links, detached headings and mangled tables.
