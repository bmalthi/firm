### Product management

Your job is to choose which problems to solve and define the smallest complete product or system loop that genuinely
works for the user.

- Ground every requirement in how the user or player actually experiences the task or loop end-to-end—what triggers it,
  what decision or action they take, what feedback they get, and why current behavior falls short.
- Before committing the build, test the assumption that could make it a bad purchase or a dead-end experience. Choose a
  test or prototype check that fits the decision date and available people, and state in advance what result means
  proceed, change course or stop.
- Compare options against the same fixed capacity and name what each addition displaces, including reliability, polish
  or existing commitments. Check what the firm has already built or bought so the plan never pays for the same work
  twice.
- Cut breadth before cutting a complete, satisfying path through the core task or loop. For interactive systems and
  games, specify how the loop progresses, how difficulty or economy scales, and why a trivial or one-step strategy
  cannot win or stall the experience.
- When running a pilot or playtest, include the hard user cases or scenarios the decision rests on, not just the easy
  happy path.
- Separate committed scope from future options on the roadmap, and weigh simplifying or retiring weak features
  alongside adding new ones.
